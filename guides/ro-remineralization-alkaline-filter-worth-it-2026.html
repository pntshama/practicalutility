Reverse Osmosis Remineralization & Alkaline Filters: Taste, Kettle Scale & pH Trade-Offs (2026)

Find out when a post-RO remineralization or alkaline stage improves coffee and drinking water taste—and when plain reverse osmosis is better for kettles and espresso machines.

Reverse osmosis membranes (0.0001 microns) strip out dissolved solids, lead, arsenic, and PFAS effectively, but they also remove naturally occurring calcium and magnesium. That leaves plain RO water slightly acidic and flat-tasting to some drinkers, which is why many under-sink and tankless systems now offer an inline remineralization or alkaline post-filter.

A remineralization stage sits after the RO membrane and dissolves small amounts of calcium and magnesium back into the water to smooth mouthfeel and nudge pH above neutral. For pour-over coffee, tea, and everyday drinking water, that light mineral balance often tastes noticeably fresher than unbuffered RO.

However, adding minerals back comes with a practical trade-off for heated appliances. Electric kettles, steam ovens, and espresso boilers run longest on low-TDS plain RO water because any added calcium can slowly form scale under heat—so choose a remineralized RO system for drinking flavor, or bypass the mineral stage when filling scale-sensitive appliances.
