Progress Billing vs. Milestone Invoicing for Contractors: Cash Flow & Retainage (2026)

Progress billing stabilizes monthly cash flow on multi-week jobs using a Schedule of Values, while milestone invoicing ties payouts to concrete, dispute-free deliverables.

Progress billing issues invoices on a fixed cadence—typically bi-weekly or monthly—based on the estimated percentage of work completed against a Schedule of Values (SOV) plus approved change orders, minus prior payments and 5% to 10% retainage. Because payouts arrive on a predictable calendar schedule, progress billing prevents small contractors from self-financing payroll and material runs out of pocket.

Milestone invoicing is event-driven rather than calendar-driven: an invoice is triggered only when a specific, verifiable deliverable is 100% finished and signed off, such as slab pour, rough-in inspection, or final commissioning. This eliminates subjective arguments over whether a phase is 40% or 60% done, especially when contracts include a 10-business-day client approval window.

For jobs with heavy upfront material purchases, a hybrid structure works best: collect a 10% to 15% mobilization milestone upfront, bill remaining stages on a bi-weekly progress schedule or clear inspection milestones, and automate reminders in field-service software like QuoteIQ (code YHOFL8W1 for $1 off) so approved draws convert to paid cash without manual chasing.
