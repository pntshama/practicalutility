Heat Pump Water Heater Anode Rod Replacement: Inspection Intervals & Powered Rod Guide (2026)

How often to inspect and replace the sacrificial anode rod in a heat pump or electric tank water heater, and when upgrading to a powered titanium rod prevents premature tank leaks.

Every steel-lined storage and heat pump water heater relies on a sacrificial anode rod to attract corrosive minerals before they eat through the tank wall. According to A.O. Smith's maintenance guidelines, standard magnesium or aluminum-zinc rods should be replaced every 3 to 5 years—or immediately if more than half of the rod's diameter has corroded away.

Hard, softened, or high-TDS water accelerates anode depletion dramatically. Bosch Home Comfort recommends inspecting the anode rod at least once a year and replacing it every two years in aggressive water conditions, noting that tank failures caused by neglected anode maintenance are typically excluded from manufacturer warranties. Discoloration, metallic taste, or a rotten-egg sulfur odor are common signs the rod is spent or reacting with sulfate-reducing bacteria.

If overhead clearance above your heat pump water heater is tight—common in basements or utility closets where the compressor sits on top of the tank—use a segmented (flexible) replacement rod or switch to a powered titanium anode rod. Pairing regular anode checks with a whole-house sediment and scale pre-filter reduces grit buildup at the bottom of the tank and keeps heat-transfer efficiency high.
