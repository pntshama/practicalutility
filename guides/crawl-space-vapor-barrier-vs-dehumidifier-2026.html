Crawl Space Vapor Barrier vs. Encapsulation & Dehumidifiers: 6-Mil vs. 12-Mil Guide (2026)

When a 6-mil or 12-mil ground vapor barrier is enough versus full crawl space encapsulation with a dedicated dehumidifier to keep relative humidity under 50%.

A basic ground vapor barrier lays polyethylene sheeting over exposed crawl space soil to block ground evaporation, whereas full encapsulation seals foundation vents, insulates stem walls, tapes all seams, and adds a dedicated dehumidifier. Building moisture specialists recommend keeping crawl space relative humidity below 60%—ideally 30% to 50%—because readings above 70% accelerate wood rot and mold growth.

While 6-mil polyethylene meets minimum code on undisturbed dirt floors, it punctures easily during plumbing or HVAC service visits. Reinforced 12-mil solid polyethylene resists tears around piers and utility runs while remaining flexible enough to wrap corners cleanly—avoiding the stiff, multi-layer adhesive separation issues sometimes reported with rigid 20-mil laminated liners.

Always fix bulk water before drying the air: extend downspouts at least 6 feet from the foundation, grade exterior soil away from stem walls, overlap barrier seams by 12 inches, and add a dedicated crawl space dehumidifier only after sealing ground vapor so the unit isn't fighting endless soil moisture.
